## 2. Positioning for 2026

### The problem with "software agency"

By 2026, generating a working CRUD application is close to free. A generic
offshore development shop is the single worst position to occupy: the price
floor is near zero and there is no defensible story.

Two things still command margin:

1. **Accountability** — someone owns the outcome, on a fixed scope and a fixed date.
2. **Hard-to-fake capability** — AI systems that survive production, data pipelines, evaluation harnesses.

### The recommended position

> **AI product engineering pods. Australian-registered, engineered in Dhaka.
> Fixed price, weekly shipping demos.**

Web, mobile and design remain on the menu as delivery capability, but they are
not the headline. AI integration and data work carry the budget in 2026, and the
Bangladesh cost base makes them extremely competitive.

### The Australia lever

Register an ABN / Pty Ltd, take an Australian business address and phone number.
Then the site can say: *Australian entity, contracts under Australian law, AUD
invoicing, engineering in Dhaka.* That one line answers most offshore
objections — legal recourse, payment rails, IP ownership, accountability — and
competitors in Dhaka cannot copy it quickly.

### Productize the offers

Three fixed offers with public prices, instead of "contact us for a quote":

| Offer | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| **AI Prototype Sprint** | 2 weeks, fixed price, working demo at the end |
| **Product Build Pod** | 6-week MVP, fixed price, weekly demos |
| **Data & Eval Pipeline** | Dataset collection, cleaning, eval harness — monthly retainer |

Publishing prices is itself the differentiator. Hiding price filters out nobody
and repels serious buyers.

**Open decision:** which single vertical to target first. Everything below
sharpens roughly threefold once that is named.

## 3. Landing page rebuild — section specification

Order matters: each section answers the next objection in the buyer's head.

1. **Hero** — outcome headline with specifics. Subhead: what you do, for whom,
   how fast, how much. Two CTAs — *Book a 20-min call* (booking embed, primary)
   and *See pricing*. Remove the Unsplash background and the parallax. Replace
   decorative code window with a real artifact: a short looping capture of
   something actually shipped.
2. **Trust strip** — "Australian entity · Engineering in Dhaka · NDA on request ·
   Fixed-price contracts". Client logos once they exist; these facts until then.
3. **Who this is for** — three ICP cards naming the buyer explicitly. Narrowness
   increases inbound, it does not reduce it.
4. **Productized offers** — three cards: price, duration, exact deliverables,
   and what lands on day 1 / week 2 / week 6.
5. **Proof** — case studies with numbers rather than screenshots. Structure:
   problem → constraint → what we built → measured result. Internal products
   qualify if the numbers are real.
6. **Process** — weekly demo cadence, shared Slack channel, staging URL from day
   3, client owns the repository from the first commit. This de-risks offshore.
7. **Team, faces visible** — re-enable the team route. Real names, GitHub,
   LinkedIn, timezone per person. An anonymous agency reads as a scam.
8. **Objection FAQ** — timezone overlap, IP ownership, payment rails
   (Wise / Payoneer / AUD invoice), what happens if the client is unhappy, who
   owns the code, what post-launch support looks like.
9. **Risk reversal** — first milestone money-back, or first week free. Stated plainly.
10. **Final CTA** — booking embed inline, not a link to `/contact`.

New routes to add: `/services/ai-integration`, `/services/data-pipelines`,
`/services/product-build`, `/pricing`, `/process`, `/blog` — each with its own
title, meta description and structured data.

## 5. Marketing — the path to the first client

Ranked by time-to-first-dollar, not by prestige.

### Weeks 1–2 — manufacture proof

- Three lighthouse projects, free or heavily discounted, in exchange for a
  written testimonial, logo rights and a metrics-based case study. All three in
  one vertical.
- Five reviews on Clutch and Upwork/Contra. Reviews are the currency cold buyers
  actually price against.

### Weeks 1–8 — outbound (highest yield for the first client)

- Personalized teardowns: 30 companies per week in one niche. Record a four-minute
  Loom auditing their product or site with two specific fixes; send it with no
  pitch in the first email. The work is already done in the video, which is why
  reply rates beat generic cold mail by an order of magnitude.
- The Australia-based co-founder runs the same play in person: Sydney/Melbourne
  startup meetups, AWS and Google Cloud user groups, coworking demo nights. A
  local face closes what cold email cannot.

### Weeks 2–12 — partnerships (fastest realistic first paid logo)

- White-label delivery for AU/US/CA design studios and marketing agencies. They
  have constant development overflow and no engineering bench.
- Target ten studio conversations. Bring a rate card and an NDA-friendly
  subcontract template.

### Ongoing — founder-led content

- Both founders post on LinkedIn three times a week. Build logs, not
  announcements: "we cut RAG latency 40% by X". Buyers follow builders.
- One deep technical article per week on `/blog`, aimed at buying-intent
  keywords: *hire AI developers Australia*, *offshore dev team Bangladesh cost*,
  *AI MVP development cost*. SEO pays out in 6–9 months — start now, do not wait on it.
- Open-source one small, genuinely useful tool. Free distribution and real proof
  of capability at the same time.

### Bangladesh track (separate funnel)

- Different buyer, different price, different pitch: SMEs, schools, clinics,
  e-commerce — digitization, POS, inventory, local payment integration.
- Channels: Facebook (dominant locally), local chambers of commerce, BASIS
  membership, university networks.
- Keep BD pricing on a separate page or gated. Never show BD rates to AU/US
  buyers, and never show AU rates to BD buyers.

### Instrumentation

One CRM or spreadsheet: source → conversation → proposal → close. After 30 days
the data says which of the six channels to double down on and which to kill.
Running all six indefinitely is how agencies stall.

**2026-09-10 — light mode repair**

The site had a broken light mode, reported from a screenshot of unreadable
headings. Cause was not colour choice but three rules competing with the
class-based theme toggle in `src/index.css`:

- `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) { body { @apply bg-dark text-light } }`
  painted the page dark from the OS setting regardless of the toggle. Because
  `.dark` was absent from `<html>`, headings kept `text-gray-900` — navy text on a
  charcoal page.
- A `@media (prefers-color-scheme: light)` block set `:root` and button colours
  off the OS setting.
- `@layer base { body { @apply bg-light } }` set a third competing background.

All three removed; the `.dark` class is now the only source of truth. Vite starter
leftovers that came with them (`a:hover { color: #646cff }`, the dark button
background) were removed at the same time.

Also fixed: navbar links were `text-white` until scroll, which is correct over a
dark hero and invisible anywhere else (the 404 page). `Navbar.tsx` now renders the
solid treatment immediately on routes without a dark hero.

Trust strip redesigned: was a dark slab that ignored the theme. Now a
theme-aware full-bleed band with contained content, mono index markers and
vertical hairlines between items.

**Technical SEO**

- `src/hooks/useSeo.ts` — the site is a client-rendered SPA with no
  SSR/prerender (open item, technical upgrade #2). This hook sets `<title>`,
  meta description, canonical URL, OG/Twitter tags and a page-specific JSON-LD
  block on every route at runtime. It helps any crawler that executes JS
  (Google, most AI answer engines) and social unfurlers that re-fetch. It is
  **not** a substitute for prerendering — a crawler reading only the initial
  HTML response still sees the homepage's tags. Applied to every page: Home,
  About, Projects, Team, Careers, Contact, NotFound (now `noindex`), and both
  blog routes.
- `scripts/generate-sitemap.mjs` — imports `blog.ts` directly (Node 24 strips
  TypeScript types natively, no build step needed) and regenerates
  `sitemap.xml` from the real route list plus every blog post. Wired into
  `npm run build` via a `prebuild` script, so it can no longer go stale the way
  the original hand-maintained sitemap did.
- `scripts/generate-rss.mjs` — same pattern, produces `public/rss.xml`. Also
  wired into `prebuild`. Linked from `index.html` via `<link rel="alternate">`.
- **`robots.txt` had two real defects, fixed:** `Disallow: /404` and
  `Disallow: /error` targeted paths that do not exist — the router is a
  catch-all (`path="*"`) with no fixed 404 URL, so those rules did nothing.
  The actual mechanism keeping bad URLs out of the index is the per-route
  `noindex` the new hook sets on `NotFound`. Separately, `Crawl-delay: 10` is
  documented to be ignored entirely by Googlebot; it only throttled Bing and
  other crawlers, working against a new site trying to get indexed quickly.
  Both removed.
